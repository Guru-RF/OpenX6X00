# The WR12 with OpenX6X00, step by step (X6200)

This page is for anyone who has a Xiegu WR12 and an X6200 running OpenX6X00, whether you got your licence last
month or have been on the air for forty years. It tells you how to connect it, how to listen with it, what the
screen tells you and why the radio will not transmit at some moments. The full detail, for those who want it,
is in the manual: [WR12 receiver (X6200)](MANUAL.md#wr12-receiver-x6200).

## What the WR12 is

The WR12 is a small receive-only converter for the X6200. With it the radio hears from 54 MHz up to 1 GHz:
FM broadcast, the airband, 2 m, 70 cm and everything in between.

It sits between your antenna and the radio and works in two ways:

- **Bypass**: the antenna goes straight through to the radio, as if the WR12 were not there. This is how it
  stays on HF and on 6 m below 54 MHz, and whenever the radio transmits. With its USB cable pulled, so without
  power, it passes the antenna through as well: an HF carrier read -72 dBm either way on ea4iln's (#134).
- **Receiving**: above 54 MHz the WR12 takes the signal, converts it down to 5.06 MHz and hands it to the
  radio. You still see and tune the real frequency (145.500 MHz, say); only the radio's own receiver works on
  5.06 MHz behind the scenes.

The WR12 can only receive. Nothing can be transmitted through it above 54 MHz, and OpenX6X00 makes sure of
that (see [Transmitting](#transmitting-with-the-wr12-connected) below).

## Connecting it

Do this with the radio off.

1. Antenna cable into the WR12's **ANT** connector.
2. A short coax from the WR12's **RIG** connector to the radio's antenna connector.
3. The WR12's USB-C cable into the radio's **HOST** port. That one cable powers it and carries its commands.

   Out of its bypass (above 54 MHz) the WR12 draws more from that port. With a hub on HOST that carries more,
   such as a USB Ethernet adapter, use a hub with its own power supply: on a bus-powered hub, and on our bench
   X6200 with the WR12 and an Ethernet adapter on one HOST port, the other device dropped out the moment the
   WR12 left its bypass.

Use an antenna that suits the band you want to hear: a 40 m dipole hears the FM broadcast band, but a 2 m
antenna will do much better on 2 m and 70 cm.

## Switching it on in OpenX6X00

1. Switch the radio on.
2. Go to **Settings > General > WR12 RECEIVER** and turn on **WR12 on HOST**. (The same switch is on the web
   configurator's Display page, Receiver card.) It is off until you turn it on, and the radio remembers it.
3. After a few seconds the screen says **"WR12 found on HOST"**, and a small **WR12** tag appears in the
   frequency strip, left of NR NB DNF. From then on the radio sets the WR12 up by itself every time it starts.

If you have no WR12 connected, leave the switch off.

## Listening

- **Band keys**: with the switch on, the bands of your band list between 54 MHz and 1 GHz come onto the band
  keys and the band plan. With the default list these are 4 m, 2 m and 70 cm.
- **Tuning**: the knob and the keypad go up to 1 GHz. Type the frequency on the keypad, or turn the knob, as on HF.
- **Mode**: the bands above 54 MHz open in **NFM**, the usual mode for repeaters and simplex on 2 m and 70 cm.
  Change it as on HF: WFM for the FM broadcast band, AM for the airband, USB or CW for SSB and CW on 2 m.
- **VFO A and B**: each VFO keeps its own frequency. You can have A on 145.500 MHz through the WR12 and B on
  7.100 MHz on HF, and switch between them with A/B: the WR12 follows the VFO in use.
- **Going back to HF**: just tune below 54 MHz or press an HF band key. The WR12 goes back to its bypass on its
  own and the radio is a normal HF radio again.
- **S-meter**: above 54 MHz it reads what the WR12 hands over, its gain included, so the S units are not those of
  HF. If you want them right, **Settings > General > CONTROLS > Per band S-meter cal** adjusts each band.

## Transmitting with the WR12 connected

**On HF and on 6 m below 54 MHz nothing changes.** The WR12 is in its bypass, the antenna goes straight through
it, and you transmit as usual, at the usual power, with PTT, the hand mic, CW, the apps, TUNE and the tuner.

**Above 54 MHz the radio is receive only**, because the WR12 cannot transmit:

- The PTT, the apps (FT8 and the others), CW, TUNE, the tuner and the SWR scan are refused, and the screen says
  why, for example "No TX on 145.500 MHz" and "WR12 receiving: no TX".
- The **hand mic's PTT**, **VOX** and the **KEY jack** reach the radio's base directly, without passing through
  OpenX6X00. While the WR12 receives, the base is held at 0 W and also told not to key anything outside its own
  amateur band table (160 to 6 m), so pressing the hand mic's PTT on 2 m does nothing at all: no TX light, no
  relay click. That is normal. If you hold the hand mic's PTT while you tune up there, the base is held at 0 W at
  once, and told about its band table as soon as you let go.
- **Right after you come back below 54 MHz**, TX stays blocked for a moment, until the WR12 confirms that it is
  back in its bypass. The screen says "WR12 not in bypass: no TX" if you press PTT in that moment. Wait a second
  and try again.

The base's own setting goes back to normal half a second after you have tuned back to where you may transmit, or
right before you transmit if that comes sooner, and at every start, so it never stays behind. If you switch WR12 on
HOST off or unplug the WR12 while tuned above 54 MHz, the radio is receive only there, so the base stays told until
you tune to a band where you may transmit.

## What the screen tells you

| On the screen | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| WR12 tag in the frequency strip | The WR12 is connected, found and set up | Nothing: all is well |
| "WR12 found on HOST" | The radio has just found and set up the WR12 | Nothing |
| "WR12 unplugged" | The WR12's USB cable was pulled out, or it lost power | Check the cable into HOST |
| "WR12 not answering" | The WR12 is connected but does not reply | Unplug it and plug it back in (dial below 54 MHz first, see below) |
| "WR12: ERROR to AT+..." | The WR12 refused a command | Same as above; if it keeps happening, report it |
| "No TX on ... MHz", "WR12 receiving: no TX" | You tried to transmit above 54 MHz | Normal: the WR12 is receive only |
| "WR12 not in bypass: no TX" | You came back below 54 MHz and the WR12 has not confirmed its bypass yet | Wait a second |
| "Base's band table not set: TX held at 0 W only" | The base did not take the extra lock; the 0 W hold still protects | Nothing urgent: the radio tries again by itself, 5 s later, then less and less often. If it stays, report it with the logs |
| "Base still keys only inside its own band table" | The base did not take the way back | The radio tries again by itself. Meanwhile you can transmit inside 160 to 6 m (60 m only 5.332 to 5.405 MHz), not on 2200 m, 630 m or 8 m. If it stays, switch the radio off and on, and report it with the logs |

The WR12's own messages and the two about the base are said once each time something goes wrong, not again and
again. The two about the base can also come without a WR12, outside the amateur bands: see
[Amateur bands only](MANUAL.md#amateur-bands-only) in the manual.

The **Amber Trey** theme draws its own display over the frequency strip, so the WR12 tag does not show there.
Everything else works the same.

## Good habits

- **Before unplugging the WR12, tune below 54 MHz or turn WR12 on HOST off.** The radio then puts the WR12 in its
  bypass first. If you pull it out while it is receiving, it cannot be put back, and whether a WR12 without
  power still lets the antenna through to the radio is not known yet: your HF signal might not reach the
  antenna.
- **Turning WR12 on HOST off while tuned above 54 MHz** moves the radio to the band below (from 2 m to the
  airband): the radio cannot receive up there without the WR12.
- **Before going back to an older version of OpenX6X00** (Rollback, or an older update), tune below 54 MHz or
  turn WR12 on HOST off. Older versions know nothing of the WR12 and would not take it out of its receive state.
- **After a restart or a crash**, OpenX6X00 puts the WR12 back in its bypass on its own, and holds TX until it has
  set the WR12 up again. You don't need to do anything.

## A transverter on the same frequencies

If you have a transverter set up (Settings > General > TRANSVERTERS) for a range above 54 MHz, the transverter
wins: on that range the radio works through the transverter, transmits on its IF, and the WR12 stays in its
bypass. To leave that range to the WR12, set the transverter's shift to 0.

## Questions

**The WR12 tag does not appear.** Check that WR12 on HOST is on and that the USB-C cable is in the HOST port (not
another USB port). Wait a few seconds after switching on. If the screen says "WR12 not answering", unplug and
plug it back in with the dial below 54 MHz.

**The network (or another USB device) drops when I tune above 54 MHz.** The WR12 draws more out of its bypass.
Put the WR12 and the other device on a hub with its own power supply.

**I hear nothing above 54 MHz.** Check the antenna cable goes into the WR12's ANT and the short coax from RIG to
the radio. Try a strong local signal first, an FM broadcast station in WFM.

**Pressing the hand mic's PTT on 2 m does nothing.** That is on purpose: the WR12 cannot transmit, and the radio
keeps its base from keying.

**Does the WR12 change anything on HF?** No. In its bypass the antenna goes straight through it, and the radio
transmits and receives on HF as without it.

**Something does not work as written here.** Reports are welcome on the project's GitHub issues, with what you
did, what the screen said, and the logs from the radio's DATA partition: the run's log and `base_i2c.log`.
