DMR

The radio listens to a DMR network's talkgroups over the internet: it logs in to one of the network's servers (its masters) as a hotspot does, and plays what the talkgroup says on the speaker at the radio's volume. Listening only: it sends the network no voice and no data, and nothing of it goes on the air. On the radio: APP > DMR, choose a talkgroup with the MFK. It logs in only while the app listens, and never at boot.

HotspotTakes effect: at the next talkgroup chosen

The network logs the radio in as a hotspot of yours: your own DMR ID (radioid.net gives one to a licensed amateur) with two digits after it, the callsign of this radio (Settings > General), which must be the one the ID belongs to, and the hotspot password the network asks for. FreeDMR takes listeners such as this one; BrandMeister keeps its hotspot protocol for hotspots with a radio of their own, so its servers are refused here (the manual, DMR). The password stays on the radio, readable by root only and out of the backups; the server gets only a proof of it. Saved, it is never shown again: the page says Set or Not set.

On the air

While the app listens: the server, the talkgroup, who talks and the last heard.

The app is not listening.

    Map

    The stations the radio heard in the last 24 hours, and the one talking now.

    DMR sends no positions, so these are approximate: each station is drawn in the country of its callsign, a little apart from the others of that country. The one talking is drawn in the city of its record at radioid.net when the radio finds that city (OpenStreetMap's place search, © OpenStreetMap contributors), else in its country. The radio asks those two only for a station it hears while this map or the app's is open, once for a station, and sends neither anything of its own. On the radio: APP > DMR, the Map button.

    Listening log

    Every station the app heard for half a second or more, kept on the radio (/mnt/dmr/heard.tsv). These are listening reports (SWL), not QSOs: nothing was on the air, and nobody was worked. A callsign opens its page on QRZ.com.

The ADIF marks every record as a listening report (SWL Y, mode DIGITALVOICE with submode DMR, propagation INTERNET, no band): a logging program keeps them apart from your QSOs. A station whose DMR ID has no callsign at radioid.net is in the CSV only. Date and time here are the radio's local time; the files have UTC.
